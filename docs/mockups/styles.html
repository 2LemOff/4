Sky blue

Why is the sky blue?

Gemini 3.5 Pro · 6 premises · 2 threads

Sunlight is a mix of every color, each a wave of a different length.

Air molecules scatter short waves far more than long ones.

↳ “Why short waves more?”
Scattering strength grows with 1/λ⁴, so halving the wavelength gives about 16× more scattering.
Ask moreCollapseBookmark

So the scattered light that reaches your eye from every direction is mostly blue.

Violet scatters even more, yet the sky isn’t violet.

↳ “Why not violet?” · 1 reply · tap to open

The sun emits less violet, and our eyes are less sensitive to it.

Why the sky is blue auto-updated

  1. Sunlight is a mix of all colors, each a different wavelength.
  2. Air molecules scatter short (blue) waves ~16× more than long ones.
  3. Violet scatters even more, but the sun emits less of it.
  4. Our eye cones are less sensitive to violet, so the mix looks blue.
  5. Scattering ∝ 1/λ⁴ because tiny charges radiate harder at high frequency. ← added from chat

Why the 4th power?
Tiny charges in each molecule shake with the light; the power they re-radiate grows with frequency⁴.

And bigger particles?
They scatter all colors about equally, which is why clouds look white.
